# Marketing Patterns

Choose the structure that best supports the visitor's decision. Follow project conventions before these patterns.

| Pattern | Use when | Common mistake | Quality check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | The page needs immediate orientation and a primary action | Large headline with no audience, value, or proof | Visitors can identify the offer and next action quickly |
| Product or service showcase | The product needs concrete explanation | Decorative mockup disconnected from the claim | Visual and copy explain a real capability together |
| Value section | Several distinct reasons to care need framing | Repeating equal feature cards regardless of importance | Group by decision relevance, not template symmetry |
| Proof | Credibility needs evidence | Invented logos, numbers, testimonials, or overly broad claims | Every claim is supplied, verifiable, or clearly modest |
| Feature detail | A visitor needs deeper understanding before action | Feature list with no user outcome | Explain who benefits and what changes for them |
| Pricing or offer | A decision depends on scope, package, or price | Forcing pricing onto pages where it is unknown | Make terms, inclusions, and CTA unambiguous when present |
| FAQ | Repeated objections can be answered concisely | Using FAQ to hide core information | Keep essential value and constraints visible in the main flow |
| CTA | The visitor is ready for the next step | Multiple equally loud actions | Establish one clear primary action and appropriate alternatives |
| Footer | Visitors need navigation, trust, or contact paths | Treating it as a dump of every link | Keep links purposeful and scannable |

## Responsive marketing flow

On narrow screens, preserve the message and CTA priority. Recompose visual/text pairings, keep important proof near its claim, avoid tiny decorative media, and ensure menus, forms, and actions are practical for touch.
