# Portfolio and Case Study Patterns

A good portfolio makes capability credible. It does not need every pattern below; use the ones supported by the work and available evidence.

| Pattern | Purpose | Avoid | Quality check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Establish who the person is, what they do, and the kind of work they seek | Generic self-description or inflated title | The visitor understands focus and character quickly |
| Selected work | Help visitors choose relevant projects to explore | Uniform thumbnail grid with no context | Each item gives a reason to open it |
| Case-study opener | Frame the project, role, and outcome | Treating a project as only a polished screenshot | Scope and contribution are clear without overclaiming |
| Challenge and context | Explain the real problem and constraints | Inventing a dramatic problem where evidence is missing | The context makes the design/engineering choices understandable |
| Process | Demonstrate judgment, collaboration, and iteration | A generic double-diamond diagram added by default | Steps reveal decisions that mattered to the outcome |
| Solution | Show the implemented experience or system | Long image gallery without annotations | Visuals are paired with useful explanation |
| Outcome | Demonstrate impact, learnings, or delivered value | Fabricated metrics or undefined success claims | Claims are factual, bounded, and attributable |
| Experience or capability | Provide supporting evidence beyond projects | Listing every technology with equal weight | Emphasize relevant strengths and actual contribution |
| Contact CTA | Offer a clear next step for the intended audience | Repeating contact buttons with no context | The action is simple, accessible, and appropriate |

## Portfolio integrity

When role, metrics, team size, client name, or project outcome is not known, do not infer it. Use neutral framing or existing copy; omit unsupported claims and flag missing evidence. A restrained, accurate portfolio creates more trust than an impressive-sounding fictional one.
