# Marketing and Portfolio Anti-AI-Slop Review

These treatments are not banned. They become generic when they are copied as decoration instead of serving the brand, message, proof, or next action.

| Pattern | Why it feels generic | Valid when | Prefer instead when not needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Huge vague hero | Consumes the first viewport without explaining value | A short, concrete message has strong visual support | A specific proposition with useful supporting context |
| Gradient, glass, blur, or glow everywhere | Substitutes effects for identity | The established brand uses it sparingly and readability remains strong | Contrast, type, image, and intentional surfaces |
| Random floating cards or blobs | Adds movement without clarifying anything | They illustrate a real product interaction or visual metaphor | One focused product or project visual |
| Repetitive two-column sections | Makes every point feel identical and templated | The content pairs naturally with a visual and benefits from repetition | Vary composition according to the content's importance |
| Generic AI copy | Sounds impressive but says nothing testable | Never as a replacement for product truth | Specific audience, problem, capability, and outcome language |
| Fake proof | Damages trust through invented logos, metrics, or testimonials | Never | Real evidence, supplied content, or no claim |
| Screenshot gallery with no context | Shows output but not thinking or contribution | A lightweight visual teaser | Project framing, role, challenge, approach, and result when available |
| CTA on every section | Creates pressure and weakens the main action | A long page has meaningful decision points | One primary path plus purposeful secondary paths |
| Oversized pills, cards, shadows, and radius | Makes a portfolio feel like a cloned SaaS template | They belong to the product's established language | Project token scale and flat grouping where appropriate |
| Motion for every element | Distracts, slows scanning, and feels performative | It explains relationship, continuity, or interaction state | Small transition, staged emphasis, or no motion |
| Decorative chart, metric, or tech badge | Implies credibility without providing evidence | It supports a genuine comparison or proof point | Concise factual explanation or real supporting detail |
| Excessive whitespace | Hides the story and makes pages feel unfinished | It creates intentional pause or emphasis | Rhythm that balances breathing room and progression |

## Quick review

Ask of each prominent treatment: does it clarify the message, demonstrate the work, strengthen trust, establish hierarchy, or guide a meaningful action? If not, remove or simplify it.
