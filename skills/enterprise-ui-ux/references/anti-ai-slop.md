# Anti-AI-Slop Review

These patterns are not forbidden. They become AI-slop when used as default decoration rather than a response to the product, task, or data.

| Pattern | Why it becomes generic | Valid when | Prefer instead when not needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Card for every section | Hides hierarchy behind repeated containers | The content is a distinct actionable or summary unit | Spacing, grouping, divider, or section heading |
| Nested cards | Creates visual noise and weakens containment | A true nested object needs its own independent action/state | One surface with internal grouping |
| Oversized radius or pills | Makes operational UI feel toy-like | Compact status, tag, or intentional project language | Project radius scale and regular controls |
| Shadow everywhere | Makes all surfaces compete and float | Dialogs, popovers, menus, and elevated overlays | Border, contrast, or spacing |
| Gradient, glass, blur, glow | Adds style without clarifying work | The established product identity supports it and readability remains strong | Flat semantic surfaces and contrast |
| Giant title or welcome banner | Consumes work area without improving task flow | A true onboarding or rare contextual milestone | Concise title plus relevant data/action |
| Generic KPI grid | Looks like a SaaS template and may hide the real workflow | Metrics drive an immediate decision | Operational queue, table, trend, alert, or summary only where useful |
| Random colors | Breaks semantic meaning and scanability | Data categories are genuinely distinct and labeled | Neutral structure plus limited semantic/accent color |
| Decorative icon or sparkle | Adds noise and weakens content hierarchy | It improves recognition for navigation, status, or a known action | Clear text, established icon, or no icon |
| Icon on every button or heading | Repeats visual noise without reducing ambiguity | The action is familiar and icon improves recognition | Text-first controls; icons only for compact known actions |
| Excessive pills and badges | Makes metadata louder than important data | Status, filter, count, or compact category needs scanning | Plain text, column, label, or grouped metadata |
| Excessive whitespace | Mimics landing pages and reduces usable density | Separation materially improves scanning or touch use | Compact-but-breathable rhythm |
| Decorative chart | Implies insight without aiding a decision | It reveals comparison, trend, distribution, or anomaly clearly | Number, trend indicator, table, or short explanation |
| Constant animation or scale hover | Distracts and can reduce trust | It communicates state, continuity, or feedback | Fast subtle state change; reduced-motion fallback |
| Marketing copy in an app | Sounds generic and delays task completion | The surface is genuinely promotional or onboarding-focused | Direct labels, status, and action-oriented copy |
| Identical repeated sections | Makes every function appear equally important | Repetition reflects a stable reusable pattern | Vary composition according to task and importance |

## Quick questions

Before retaining a treatment, ask: does it help users find information, understand status, compare data, perform an action, recover from an error, or understand hierarchy? If the answer is no, remove or simplify it.
